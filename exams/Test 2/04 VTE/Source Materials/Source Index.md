## Objective-driven study priorities

1. Explain normal hemostasis and how Virchow's triad promotes pathologic clot formation.
2. Recognize DVT and PE using symptoms, vital-sign changes, oxygenation, and diagnostic data.
3. Differentiate localized DVT from embolization to the pulmonary circulation.
4. Prioritize rapid action for sudden dyspnea, hypoxemia, hypotension, confusion, syncope, or arrest.
5. Apply safe DVT/PE nursing care, including no massage, prescribed activity/bedrest, oxygen/support, anticoagulation, and bleeding precautions.
6. Match heparin and warfarin to monitoring and reversal; evaluate DOAC safety using the instructor's expectations.
7. Evaluate whether therapy prevents clot extension/embolization without causing bleeding.

## Highest-risk exam clue

A patient with known or suspected DVT who develops sudden respiratory or circulatory change may have a PE. Breathing and circulation become the immediate priority.
