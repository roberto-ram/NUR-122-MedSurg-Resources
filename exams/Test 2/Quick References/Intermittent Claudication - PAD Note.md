# Intermittent Claudication - PAD Quick Note

## Definition

Intermittent claudication is **ischemic muscle pain caused by peripheral artery disease (PAD)**. It usually affects the calf but may occur in the thigh or buttock, depending on where arterial narrowing is located.

## Why it happens

Arterial narrowing → reduced blood flow to the exercising muscle → oxygen supply cannot meet increased demand → ischemic pain or cramping.

Rest decreases the muscle's oxygen demand → available blood flow becomes adequate again → pain improves, usually within several minutes.

## Classic assessment pattern

- Reproducible aching, cramping, heaviness, or fatigue with walking or exercise
- Usually occurs after a predictable amount of activity
- Improves with rest
- May occur with cool skin, diminished pulses, delayed capillary refill, shiny skin, hair loss, or pallor with elevation

## Important distinction

- **Intermittent claudication:** Pain occurs with activity and improves with rest.
- **Ischemic rest pain:** Pain occurs without activity, often in the foot or toes, and may worsen with elevation. This suggests more severe arterial insufficiency and is more concerning.
- **Chronic venous insufficiency discomfort:** Often aching or heaviness with edema that improves when the leg is elevated.

## Nursing and exam priorities

- Assess the pain pattern, pulses, skin temperature/color, capillary refill, wounds, and activity tolerance.
- Encourage a prescribed walking program: walk until moderate discomfort occurs, rest until it resolves, then resume.
- Promote smoking cessation and management of diabetes, blood pressure, and cholesterol.
- Protect the feet from injury and inspect them daily because impaired arterial flow delays healing.
- Do not assume compression is safe merely because edema is present; significant arterial insufficiency must be evaluated first.

## Exam clue

**Walking → calf pain → rest → relief = intermittent claudication from PAD.**

## One-line takeaway

Intermittent claudication means the leg receives enough arterial blood at rest but not enough to meet the increased oxygen demand of exercise.
