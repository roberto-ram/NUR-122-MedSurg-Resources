# Test 2 Objective-Based Priority Map

This map comes from the official NUR 122 exemplars and objectives. It defines the highest-priority learning targets for study guides, quizzes, case studies, and exam review.

## How priority is determined

For every exemplar, be able to:

1. **Explain** the normal physiology and what changed
2. **Recognize** assessment findings and diagnostic evidence
3. **Differentiate** conditions that look similar
4. **Act** with safe, prioritized nursing interventions
5. **Evaluate** whether treatment worked and recognize complications early

## Lecture Day 7 — Coronary Perfusion

**Exemplars:** CAD, chronic stable angina, ACS, unstable angina, MI.

**Highest-priority targets:**

- Trace coronary circulation and explain myocardial oxygen supply versus demand.
- Connect atherosclerosis and coronary obstruction to ischemia, angina, and myocardial injury.
- Recognize chest-pain patterns, ECG changes, cardiac biomarkers, and other signs of impaired coronary perfusion.
- Differentiate stable angina, unstable angina, and MI from presentation through treatment response.
- Prioritize immediate and long-term nursing care for coronary ischemia and ACS.
- Evaluate response to medications/reperfusion and recognize time-sensitive complications.

**Core exam comparison:** stable angina vs unstable angina vs MI.

## Lecture Day 8 — Peripheral Perfusion

**Exemplars:** PVD, PAD, chronic venous insufficiency.

**Highest-priority targets:**

- Explain arterial delivery and venous return in normal systemic circulation.
- Connect impaired arterial inflow or venous return to pulse, pain, skin, temperature, edema, and wound findings.
- Recognize assessment and diagnostic evidence of impaired peripheral perfusion.
- Differentiate PAD from CVI, especially pain pattern, pulses, skin changes, ulcers, edema, and positioning.
- Select safe nursing interventions, including when compression or elevation helps or creates risk.
- Evaluate improved perfusion/wound healing and recognize limb-threatening deterioration.

**Core exam comparison:** PAD vs CVI.

## Lecture Day 9 — Heart Failure

**Exemplar:** congestive heart failure.

**Highest-priority targets:**

- Explain ventricular filling, ejection, cardiac output, and normal chamber function.
- Connect impaired pumping to reduced cardiac output and pulmonary/systemic congestion.
- Recognize assessment findings and diagnostics associated with heart failure.
- Distinguish left-sided, right-sided, acute, and worsening manifestations when supported by course materials.
- Prioritize interventions that support oxygenation, cardiac output, perfusion, and fluid balance.
- Evaluate response using respiratory status, perfusion, weight/fluid trends, and relevant diagnostics.
- Recognize progression, dysrhythmias, and acute pulmonary edema early.

**Core priority:** breathing and perfusion deterioration from pump failure.

## Lecture Day 10 — Venous Thromboembolism

**Exemplars:** VTE, DVT, PE.

**Highest-priority targets:**

- Explain how a venous clot forms and impairs blood flow and tissue perfusion.
- Recognize risk factors, DVT findings, PE findings, and diagnostic evidence.
- Differentiate a localized DVT from an embolized clot causing pulmonary obstruction.
- Prioritize VTE prevention and safe care for a suspected or confirmed clot.
- Respond rapidly to sudden respiratory or circulatory deterioration suggesting PE.
- Monitor anticoagulation, bleeding risk, and evidence that treatment is effective.

**Core exam comparison:** DVT vs PE.

## Lecture Day 11 — Infection

**Exemplars:** pneumonia, influenza, cellulitis, MRSA.

**Highest-priority targets:**

- Explain normal host defenses and the inflammatory response to infection.
- Recognize assessment findings and diagnostic data for respiratory and skin/soft-tissue infections.
- Differentiate pneumonia, influenza, cellulitis, and MRSA by presentation and transmission pattern.
- Choose appropriate precautions and interventions that limit transmission.
- Prioritize gas exchange for respiratory infection and tissue integrity/perfusion for cellulitis.
- Evaluate antimicrobial/supportive treatment and recognize worsening infection or sepsis.

**Core exam comparison:** respiratory vs skin infection, including transmission and precautions.

## Lecture Day 12 — GI Inflammation

**Exemplars:** GERD, pancreatitis, appendicitis, hepatitis A–E.

**Highest-priority targets:**

- Explain how inflammation disrupts normal esophageal, pancreatic, appendiceal, or hepatic function.
- Recognize characteristic assessment findings and diagnostic evidence for each exemplar.
- Differentiate GERD, pancreatitis, appendicitis, and hepatitis by pathophysiology and presentation.
- Prioritize interventions that support recovery and prevent condition-specific complications.
- Evaluate response to medical, nutritional, procedural, and supportive treatment.
- Recognize urgent complications such as bleeding/aspiration, severe pancreatitis effects, appendix rupture/peritonitis, or liver dysfunction.
- Compare hepatitis A–E by transmission, expected course/chronicity, prevention, and teaching.

**Core exam comparisons:** pancreatitis vs appendicitis; hepatitis A–E.
